APARN doesn’t need to borrow legitimacy from institutions built for text-based, Western academic research. It can build its own.

The Mechanism

Zenodo — an open-science repository, free, with real DOIs.

  • Archive the back-catalogue — every APARN meeting from #1 to the most recent, uploaded and permanently preserved.
  • From APARN 2027 — new submissions accepted under a defined publication track, each issued a DOI, including non-textual work.
  • No cost — Zenodo is free and openly maintained by CERN / OpenAIRE.

This already works — elsewhere

The NIME precedent

  • Open governance — the NIME Board replaced a closed Steering Committee; anyone who attended one of the last 5 conferences can nominate and vote.
  • A working Zenodo pipeline — a Proceedings Officer role, and a public tool that uploads proceedings and issues DOIs every year.
  • A reviewing culture — 3 reviewers plus a meta-reviewer per submission, open to anyone in the community.
  • Tested this summer — as Demo & Poster Chair for NIME 2026, I helped run exactly this process: artistic installations, peer-reviewed, issued DOIs as non-traditional research output.

Keeping 300+ members engaged

A quarterly gathering, adapted from SIGGRAPH DAC’s SPARKS.

SPARKS runs monthly lightning talks + discussion for the SIGGRAPH community between annual conferences. For APARN’s first year, the same idea — scaled to quarterly, open call for presenters, starting shortly after the Singapore 2026 meeting. Recordings feed directly into the Zenodo archive.
